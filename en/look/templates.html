Templates

A template decides how every slide looks: layouts, colors, fonts, logos and pictures.

This page covers what a template is, the six templates that come with banners, and the options that a template of your own can set.

One line changes the whole deck

A template draws every slide of the deck. The slides only hold their content (titles, text, plots); the template decides where each piece goes and which colors and fonts it uses. That is why one call changes the look of a whole presentation:

from banners import EXECUTIVE, configure

configure(template=EXECUTIVE)

Every slide created after this call uses EXECUTIVE. Without a call to configure(template=...), banners uses Template(), the default template.

The built-in templates

All of them are importable from banners:

from banners import COSMOS, EXECUTIVE, INSTITUTIONAL, TERRA, VIBRANT, Template

What a template is made of

A Template combines three things:

  • Layouts: where the title, the content, the logo and the bottom bar go on each kind of slide. They are built into banners; the Layouts page shows how a slide picks one.
  • A Palette: the colors, by role (title, text, background, bar…). See Palettes.
  • A Theme: fonts, title weight, icon stroke and a size multiplier. See Themes.

On top of those, a template can carry logos, pictures and a few switches:

Option What it does
logo_on_dark, logo_on_light Logo drawn on dark slides (cover, closing) and on light ones. A URL, a file path or raw bytes.
mark Small symbol in the top-right corner of content slides: an image or an icon name such as "chart".
cover_image, divider_image Photo used by every cover, or every divider, unless the slide sets its own.
divider_icon Large icon on dividers without a photo.
closing_image Picture next to the closing title.
bar, bar_split Draw the two-color bar along the bottom edge, and how much of it is the primary color.
auto_contrast Replace a title color that would be hard to read on its background. Turn it off to keep exact brand colors.

Images given as URLs are loaded by the browser when the slide is shown; paths and bytes are embedded in the slide.

A template of your own

A template is a plain Python object, so a deck can define its own in a notebook cell and use it with configure():

from banners import Palette, Template, Theme, configure

ours = Template(
    palette=Palette(primary="#0f766e", secondary="#f59e0b"),
    theme=Theme(heading_font="Georgia, serif"),
    mark="chart",
)
configure(template=ours, team="Data Office", date="2026")

Your organization’s template builds a complete one, with logos.

Changing only the colors or the fonts

To keep a template’s layouts and change only its colors or fonts, pass a palette or a theme to configure() instead of building a new template:

from banners import BLUE, COSMOS, configure

configure(template=COSMOS, palette=BLUE)

The palette given to configure() replaces the template’s palette as a whole. The next pages explain palettes and themes in detail.
